# Skill: Ship 30 for 30 Atomic Essay Engine

## 1. Overview & Writing Philosophy
The **Ship 30 for 30** writing system, created by Dickie Bush & Nicolas Cole, transforms complex domain expertise into highly engaging, skimmable, and actionable long-form atomic essays.

This skill synthesizes grounded insights from *Lenny's Podcast* transcripts into a structured, authoritative essay (~1,250 words) that product managers, founders, and growth leaders can immediately read, share, and implement.

---

## 2. Core Architectural Principles

### A. The Atomic Hook
- Open with a bold, curiosity-inducing statement that challenges conventional PM wisdom.
- Never start with generic filler (e.g., "In today's fast-paced world...").
- State the stakes immediately: why does ignoring this principle lead to failure?

### B. The 1-3-1 Rhythm
- Structure paragraphs to maintain cognitive velocity:
  - **1 sentence:** A punchy, standalone statement.
  - **3 sentences:** Rapid-fire explanation, data point, or transcript example.
  - **1 sentence:** The golden takeaway or transition.

### C. Scannable Visual Architecture
- Use clear Markdown headings (`### Step 1: ...`, `### 2. ...`).
- Use bullet points and numbered lists for multi-step execution.
- Use **selective bolding** on metrics, definitions, and action verbs so a reader scanning in 15 seconds captures 80% of the value.

### D. Strict Transcript Grounding
- Ground every framework, case study, and metric directly in the retrieved podcast transcripts.
- Explicitly credit the guest (e.g., Rahul Vohra, Elena Verna, Brian Balfour, Shreyas Doshi).
- Do not invent hypothetical metrics not present in the evidence.

### E. The "One Big Takeaway"
- Conclude with a single, memorable, actionable maxim summarizing the core thesis.
